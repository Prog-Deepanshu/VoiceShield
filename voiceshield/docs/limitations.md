# Empirical Model Limitations & Benchmark Analysis

This document provides a technical appraisal of the operational limitations of the VoiceGuard AASIST model and explains why automated systems must not treat deepfake scores as definitive legal proof of fraud.

---

## 1. The ASVspoof 2019 Domain Shift Challenge

### Benchmark Provenance
The AASIST model was trained on the **ASVspoof 2019 Logical Access (LA)** dataset:
- Audio recordings were gathered in controlled acoustic environments.
- Uniform $16\text{ kHz}$ sample rate, 16-bit PCM.
- Synthetic voices were generated by text-to-speech (TTS) and voice conversion (VC) algorithms from 2019 (e.g. WaveNet, Tacotron 2, classic neural vocoders).

### Real-World Benchmark Findings
In the original VoiceGuard repository, the author evaluated AASIST against an out-of-distribution modern benchmark (`UniDataPro/real-vs-fake-human-voice-deepfake-audio`), recorded in `benchmark_dataset_report.txt`:

```
===================================
  VoiceGuard Dataset Benchmark Summary:
===================================
Total Real (Human): 39 -> Accuracy: 12.8%
Total Fake (Synthetic): 11 -> Accuracy: 36.4%
-----------------------------------
Overall Accuracy: 18.0% (9/50)
```

**Critical finding**: **$87.2\%$ of authentic human recordings were incorrectly classified as `MEDIUM` or `HIGH` risk.**

### Root Causes of Domain Shift:
1. **High Sample Rate Downsampling**:
   Modern smartphones and studio microphones record at $44.1\text{ kHz}$ or $48\text{ kHz}$. Downsampling to $16\text{ kHz}$ introduces steep low-pass filtering. To a model trained only on native $16\text{ kHz}$ speech, this spectral cutoff mimics vocoder filtering artifacts.
2. **Audio Codec Compression**:
   Cellular networks (AMR, AMR-WB, EVS) and VoIP codecs (Opus, G.711, G.722) perform lossy perceptual compression. Quantization noise and harmonic stripping in lossy codecs trigger high deepfake scores on real human voices.
3. **Background Acoustic Environments**:
   Room reverberation, background fan noise, or background music alter the spectral centroid and flatness, causing false positives.

## 2. Ethical and Operational Safeguards in VoiceShield

To prevent false accusations, VoiceShield enforces four architectural safeguards:

### 1. The `UNCERTAIN` Classification Tier
VoiceShield refuses to force a binary "Human vs. AI" verdict when model scores fall in the intermediate zone ($0.30 - 0.70$). Inconclusive audio is explicitly flagged as `UNCERTAIN`.

### 2. Multi-Window Temporal Aggregation
Rather than judging a 30-second call on the introductory 4 seconds (which often contains ringing, breath noise, or greeting latency), VoiceShield runs overlapping sliding windows across the full audio timeline.

### 3. Explainability via Spectral Diagnostics
Alongside the raw model score, VoiceShield computes:
- **Pitch Stability Index**: Natural human speech exhibits continuous micro-intonations; synthetic voices frequently exhibit artificial pitch smoothing.
- **Spectral Flatness**: Measures vocoder noise floor elevation.
- **Harmonic Ratio**: Verifies vocal tract resonance integrity.

### 4. Non-Accusatory Classification
VoiceShield labels detections as `LIKELY AI-GENERATED` or `LIKELY HUMAN`. It does **not** label callers as "scammers" or "fraudsters". A synthetic voice may be used legitimately (e.g. speech disability assistive devices, synthesized automated IVR prompts, authorized voice avatars).

---

## 3. Production Deployment Recommendations for Call Centers

1. **Never Terminate Calls Automatically**:
   Use VoiceShield as a tiered alert system for human fraud analysts, not an automated call-dropping switch.
2. **Require Multi-Factor Verification**:
   When an alert triggers, prompt the caller with out-of-band step-up authentication (e.g. mobile banking push notification or SMS OTP) rather than interrogating them directly.
3. **Calibrate Thresholds to Channel Quality**:
   If deploying on standard PSTN telephony (8kHz narrowband audio), adjust the `threshold_high` threshold to $\ge 0.75$ to absorb telephone channel compression noise.
